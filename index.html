Why Poland?

Europe's rising star for ambitious students.

Poland is one of the fastest-growing destinations for international students in the European Union. With part-time work that carries no weekly cap, a nine-month permit to find work after graduation and an EU-recognised qualification, it is the ideal place to study and build a career.

World-ranked universities

Twenty Polish institutions feature in the QS World University Rankings 2027 — from Warsaw and Kraków to Gdańsk and Wrocław.

02 EU education

Affordable, EU-recognised degrees

Some of the most accessible public-university study in the European Union, with qualifications recognised right across it.

03 Graduate permit

Nine months to launch your career

Stay on after graduation to find work or start a business — no sponsor required, and graduates of full-time Polish degrees need no work permit.

04 Work rights

Work with no weekly cap

Full-time students at approved or exempt universities need no work permit and face no hour limit. Part-time, postgraduate and MBA study do not qualify.

05 Practical learning

Diverse curriculum, hands-on teaching

A distinctive, diverse curriculum with a high level of practical instruction — for students from every stream.

06 Research

Doctoral schools that fund you

Polish doctoral schools charge no tuition and award a scholarship to every full-time doctoral candidate.

    Settle in Poland

    Your journey doesn't end at graduation.

    Poland offers a structured staircase for international students: study, stay on, work, settle. Each step builds on the last, and we plan the whole climb with you from day one.

    Complete a full-time degree at a university approved to admit international students. We help you choose programmes that lead straight into the graduate permit.

    • A national visa to arrive, then a residence permit for studies; your first permit runs 15 months
    • Part-time work with no work permit and no weekly cap while you study full-time
    • Your visa file: admission letter, proof of funds, health insurance and accommodation

    We help: an MSWiA-approved shortlist, and a visa file checked the way a consular officer reads it.

    Everything you need to know about studying, financing and settling in Poland.

    Still curious?

    Ask Tutee AI for an instant answer, or talk to a counsellor for free.

    You need a valid passport, strong academic transcripts, proof of English for an English-taught degree (IELTS Academic, TOEFL iBT or PTE Academic), academic references and a compelling motivation letter. There is no national entrance exam and no central application system — you apply to each university through its own recruitment system, to its own deadlines. Some postgraduate courses, MBAs in particular, may also ask for a GMAT score or work experience.

    First we check that your university is approved by the Ministry of the Interior to admit foreigners, or exempt from that requirement — without it a residence permit cannot be issued. Once you have the admission decision you apply at the Polish consulate for a national visa (type D), showing your funds, travel medical insurance and accommodation. After you arrive you file for the temporary residence permit for studies through the MOS system and attend the voivodeship office in person. We guide you through every step.

    Yes! We have exclusive tie-ups with leading financial institutions and banks for both collateral and non-collateral education loans. These can cover your tuition, accommodation and living expenses with competitive interest rates.

    Yes, but not immediately — and the timing is the part that matters. Family reunification requires you to have lived in Poland for at least two years on consecutive temporary residence permits, with your current permit issued for at least a year, so it cannot be done from your first permit, which runs 15 months. By your second permit a bachelor's or master's student does qualify, and your partner's permit then carries the right to work with no separate work permit. We build that timeline into your plan from the start.

    Poland grants a graduate a temporary residence permit to look for work or start a business once, for nine months. You must apply in person while you are still legally resident — at the latest on the last day of your lawful stay — so do not leave the country first. The permit does not itself carry a work right; what lets you take a job is a separate rule, that graduates of full-time studies at Polish universities need no work permit. There is no sponsorship requirement, which makes it the ideal bridge into an EU Blue Card.

    Yes. We partner with student housing providers to secure safe, comfortable living spaces near your campus, and run pre-departure briefings covering Polish culture, academic expectations, packing lists and immigration procedures.
